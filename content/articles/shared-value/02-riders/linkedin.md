## Post

Getting around should leave room for a life.

A class to get to. A customer to meet. An appointment you cannot miss.

A fare changes what is left for lunch, business supplies or another essential trip. An extra connection can turn a manageable outing into one you have to reconsider.

LEADERSHIP's 13 September reporting made that concrete: the Atan–Ojuore fare in Ogun had risen from ₦1,000 to ₦1,200. One route, one reported change—and an extra ₦200 for the person making that journey.

In TagRides' early survey of 210 Lagos riders, 192 named pricing as a priority, 153 named convenience and 147 named safety.

That is our company research sample. It gave us a clear brief: an affordable fare is useful only when the journey works around it.

We are building TagRides to help riders find drivers already heading their way, agree a fare and share a suitable part of the journey.

We think about the full trip: the shared fare, connecting fares, walking, waiting and knowing who you are travelling with.

Along also lets riders keep accepted alternatives available before boarding, where compatible drivers still have space. An agreed fare is not an advance seat reservation.

The goal is a journey that fits your purpose and budget, and still makes sense when you need it again.

Our MVP is in internal testing as we prepare a focused Lagos pilot. We want to learn from journeys to school, work, places of business and services—as well as family visits and the other trips that make a life.
