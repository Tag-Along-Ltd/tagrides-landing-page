# LinkedIn — Drivers and livelihoods

Voice: founder · Editorial draft · Website first, LinkedIn afterwards

## Post

A cancelled pickup disappears from a screen.
The fuel spent reaching it does not return to the tank.

For a driver, that is money, time and another decision about whether the next trip will be worthwhile.

On 13 September, Nairametrics reported petrol at ₦1,400–₦1,430 per litre at most Lagos stations it checked. At ₦1,400, 20 litres costs ₦28,000. Those are dated reported prices, but the pressure behind the driver's daily decisions is clear.

TagRides starts with a different question: who needs a seat on the journey this driver already plans to make?

Our Along model connects riders with drivers travelling compatible routes. They agree a fare for the stretch they share, with pickup along the driver's route.

The goal is practical: make available passenger space useful while keeping the driver's journey at the centre.

But more fares are only part of the story. Fuel, platform charges, vehicle wear and time still matter. The useful number is what the driver keeps after those costs.

That is what we want to learn from our planned Lagos pilot: completed paid rides, worthwhile driver earnings, manageable pickups and people choosing to offer seats again.

Our MVP is in internal testing. The first proof should be something a driver recognises at the end of an ordinary day: the journey still worked, and sharing it was worthwhile.

Drive a repeat route, own passenger vehicles or organise a driver community? I'd like to hear about your corridor and usual departure time.
