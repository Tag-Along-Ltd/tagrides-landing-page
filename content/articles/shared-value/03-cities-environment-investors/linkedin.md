## Post

The next transport breakthrough may already have a seat.

A driver is going somewhere. Nearby, another person needs to travel in much the same direction. The missing piece is a useful connection.

That is the idea behind TagRides: help people find and share suitable passenger space on an existing journey.

On 5 September, LAMATA reported more than nine million Blue Line passenger movements over three years. That is a reminder of the scale of everyday transport—and why useful connections to mass transit matter.

For a city, the opportunity is more useful travel from capacity already moving. For a rider, it could be a manageable fare or a connection to public transport. For a driver, a journey worth sharing.

The environmental case depends on three questions:

Was the driver already making the trip?
How much extra driving did the pickup add?
What would the rider otherwise have used?

Research supports exploring shared capacity. But a full vehicle alone does not prove lower traffic or emissions. Those outcomes need local evidence.

The investment case is just as practical. Do riders return at ordinary fares? Do drivers find the earnings worthwhile? Can the service provide dependable pickups and cover its operating costs?

Our MVP is in internal testing. A focused Lagos pilot is the next step towards answering those questions.

The long-term ambition is mobility useful to hundreds of millions, and eventually billions, of people. The next proof is closer: people on one route choosing to share the journey again.
